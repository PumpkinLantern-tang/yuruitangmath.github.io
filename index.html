About

I am a researcher in graph theory. My research interests include extremal graph theory, graph subdivisions, Hamiltonicity, expansion, cycles and paths, and Turán-type problems.

Research Interests

Extremal Graph Theory

Turán-type problems, forbidden subgraphs, generalized Turán numbers, and sharp extremal constructions.

Graph Subdivisions

Spanning and balanced subdivisions, topological minors, and density conditions forcing prescribed subdivision structures.

Hamiltonicity & Expansion

Hamilton cycles, long cycles, robust and sublinear expansion, regular graphs, and degree thresholds.

Cycles & Paths

Consecutive cycle lengths, circumference, longest paths and cycles, and transversal-type questions.

Probabilistic Methods

Random graph constructions, concentration inequalities, pseudorandomness, and probabilistic extremal examples.

Structural Graph Theory

Connectivity, separators, tree decompositions, regularity, and structural obstructions to spanning configurations.

Example notation. I often study questions involving graphs such as \(K_{s,t}\)-free graphs, subdivisions of \(H\), and extremal quantities such as \(\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\).

1 Sparse graphs with an independent or foresty minimum vertex cut Kun Cheng, Yurui Tang, Xingzhi Zhan Published Discrete Mathematics 349 (2026), 114658

Abstract

A connected graph is called fragile if it contains an independent vertex cut. In 2002 Chen and Yu proved that every connected graph of order \(n\) and size at most \(2n-4\) is fragile, and in 2013 Le and Pfender characterized the non-fragile graphs of order \(n\) and size \(2n-3\). It is natural to consider minimum vertex cuts. We prove two results. (1) Every connected graph of order \(n\) with \(n\ge 7\) and size at most \(\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor\) has an independent minimum vertex cut; (2) every connected graph of order \(n\) with \(n\ge 7\) and size at most \(2n\) has a foresty minimum vertex cut. Both results are best possible.

2 The maximum number of cliques in graphs with given fractional matching number and minimum degree Chengli Li, Yurui Tang Published Discrete Applied Mathematics 379 (2026), 390–399

Abstract

Recently, Ma, Qian and Shi determined the maximum size of an \(n\)-vertex graph with given fractional matching number \(s\) and maximum degree at most \(d\). Motivated by this result, we determine the maximum number of \(\ell\)-cliques in a graph with given fractional matching number and minimum degree, which generalizes Shi and Ma's result about the maximum size of a graph with given fractional matching number and minimum degree at least one. We also determine the maximum number of complete bipartite graphs in a graph with prescribed fractional matching number and minimum degree.
